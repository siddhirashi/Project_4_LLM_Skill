# Sample Input: Research Instrument Auditor

Use this file to demonstrate the skill. The questionnaire below is intentionally imperfect. It is fictional and contains no real participant data.

## Research brief

**Topic:** Social media influencer promotions and college students' purchasing behaviour.

**Research objective:** Explore how college students encounter influencer promotions and whether those promotions are related to product consideration and purchasing behaviour.

**Target respondents:** Students aged 18 or above currently enrolled in a college or university.

**Mode:** Online questionnaire.

**Concepts of interest:** Exposure to promotions, attention to promoted products, product consideration, purchase behaviour and trust in influencer recommendations.

## Draft questionnaire to audit

1. Do you agree that Instagram influencers manipulate students into buying unnecessary products?
   - Yes
   - No

2. How often do you use social media?
   - Rarely
   - Sometimes
   - Often
   - Very often

3. Do you follow influencers and buy products they recommend?
   - Yes
   - No

4. How much time do you spend on Instagram and YouTube every day?
   - Less than 1 hour
   - 1–2 hours
   - 2–3 hours
   - More than 3 hours

5. Which influencer content affects you the most?
   - Fashion
   - Beauty
   - Technology
   - Food

6. Have you ever purchased a product after seeing it promoted by an influencer?
   - Yes
   - No

7. What is your monthly family income?
   - Below ₹25,000
   - ₹25,000–₹50,000
   - ₹50,000–₹1,00,000
   - Above ₹1,00,000

## Copy-ready test prompt

Act as the Research Instrument Auditor. Review the questionnaire above using the research brief. Do not simply correct grammar. Identify leading, loaded, ambiguous, double-barrelled, assumptive or difficult-to-answer questions; check whether the questions measure the stated concepts; examine the response options and question order; and flag unnecessary sensitive data collection. For each issue, explain why it matters and suggest a revision that preserves the intended meaning. Distinguish high-, medium- and low-severity concerns. Do not claim the questionnaire is valid or reliable. Finish with a short pilot-testing checklist and list any decisions I, as the researcher, still need to make.

## What a good audit should notice

- Q1 is leading and loaded (“manipulate”, “unnecessary”) and assumes a negative effect.
- Q2 uses vague frequency labels without a defined time period.
- Q3 combines following influencers with buying their recommendations.
- Q4 combines two platforms, so the answer cannot show time spent on each separately; its time bands also need clear boundaries and a time frame.
- Q5 forces one content category and omits “other” or “none”; “affects” is not defined.
- Q6 is clearer, but a time period may be needed and the answer does not establish causation.
- Q7 asks for sensitive household financial information without an obvious explanation of why it is necessary; the student should justify, remove or redesign it and consult relevant ethics guidance.

These are expected observations, not a predetermined final answer. The skill should explain context-dependent decisions and may identify additional issues.
